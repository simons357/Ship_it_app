## The tea-cup picture

Imagine hot tea.

- Sharp swirls want to stay sharp (that’s the **dangerous stretch**).
- As the tea cools, viscosity **melts** the hard edges.
- But even after the tea cools, the **shape** of the swirl still matters.

Lemma★ asks: for every smooth “cup,” does that shape-danger stay bounded by one finite number?

![Tea cup — viscosity melts; shape remains](./assets/lemma-campaign/01-tea-cup-viscosity-melts.png)

## The tug-of-war

Two teams pull a rope:

| Team | What it is | What it wants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stretch** (\(T_c\)) | nonlinear twisting | blowup |
| **Spread** (\(\mathcal{D}_s\)) | energy spread across shells | safety |

The score in the middle is **\(\mathcal{R}_\star\)** (R-star).

Lemma★ says: stretch cannot beat spread by more than one universal constant — **for every smooth shape**.

If that were proved, this packaging would close the Millennium fluids problem.  
It is **not** proved.

![Tug-of-war — stretch vs spread](./assets/lemma-campaign/03-tug-of-war-stretch-vs-spread.png)

## Shape is not size

Turn the volume up. Make the fluid “bigger.” Change viscosity.

The Lemma★ score should **not** care. Size cancels. Viscosity cancels.  
What’s left is **shape**.

That’s why the campaign talks about a “barycenter” — the center of shape-space — not a louder number.
