# Theorem A — what to do with it

The sitting write-up is `tex/theorem-a-q1.tex`.
This is **not** legal advice and not a patent strategy.

---

## What you have

A dated, typed record that the \(Q_1\) system
(\(\varepsilon>0\), \(\beta\ge 1/2\)) is globally regular
on \(\mathbb{T}^3\). That is Ladyzhenskaya class in your
notation. It is **not** unaugmented Navier–Stokes.

You cannot patent the estimate. You can timestamp
**your write-up** and keep the claim honest.

---

## What to do (order)

1. **Keep the title honest.**  
   Use: *Global regularity for the \(Q_1\)-augmented
   Navier–Stokes system (this PDE only).*  
   Do **not** use: solved NS, smoothness and existence,
   unaugmented, Millennium, prize.

2. **Zenodo is the right first public box.**  
   You already deposit there. Upload the compiled PDF
   plus the `.tex`. You get a DOI and a date. That is
   a public timestamp of *this note*, not a journal
   acceptance and not a close of Track B.

3. **A reader who knows the class, before arXiv.**  
   A fluids / PDE person (teacher, colleague) should
   confirm the framing: this records a known class,
   credits Ladyzhenskaya and Málek–Nečas–Růžička, and
   does not claim classical NS. That is the protection
   that matters: you do not get stuck to an overclaim.

4. **arXiv only if the note stays in that frame.**  
   math.AP will read it as a short note, not a new
   existence theory. You will likely need an endorser.
   Do not send it as classical regularity. If a reader
   says the only content is the standard class plus
   notation, that is a reason to **stop at Zenodo**.

5. **Do not send Track B or RH as if they sat.**  
   Those WRITE lines are still open. Depositing them
   as closes is the refuse.
