## 1. Executive summary

**TITAN-X** is a free-flight **stratospheric aerostat** that sits in the altitude band between conventional aircraft and satellites. It carries a **20,000 lb modular payload**, stays aloft indefinitely on **helium buoyancy + solar power**, and reconfigures by exchanging gondolas and underbelly modules.

**The ask shape (program):** fund a staged pathfinder → first full-scale hull → initial regional / California firefighting nodes → fleet scale toward a planning **12-unit** base by end of decade.

**Why it matters to capital:** pre-positioned regional coverage converts disaster and wildfire response from **days into hours**, while civil and commercial utilization is intended to underwrite year-round availability. One hull family serves firefighting, disaster, infrastructure, communications, science, rockoon launch, and selected dual-use defense missions — a shared cost base instead of separate fleets.

## 2. Problem

- Wildfire and disaster response still depends on scarce surge aircraft and ground logistics. Assets arrive late; coverage collapses when weather or tanker availability fails.
- Satellites persist but cannot deliver mass, retardant, or rapid modular logistics. Aircraft deliver mass but not indefinite station-keeping.
- Cities and critical infrastructure need **hours-scale** airborne nodes for observation, temporary communications, and logistics — not ad-hoc charter after the event.
- Dual-use buyers want persistent ISR / relay / forward logistics without owning a unique airframe for every mission.

**Gap:** nothing practical today combines **indefinite loiter**, **school-bus-class payload**, **modular mission swap**, and **near-zero persistent ops carbon** in the aircraft–satellite band.
