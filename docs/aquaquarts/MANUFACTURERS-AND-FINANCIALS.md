# AquaQuarts — Manufacturers & financial analysis

**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Status:** Planning model — **get written quotes** before spending. Numbers below are ranges from public OEM norms + luxury hospitality craft, not signed bids.  
**True BOM challenge:** Solid carved stone + food-safe quartz lining + fused purple glass is a **hybrid** — few factories do all three in one SKU. Plan a **lead maker + glass partner** (or studio that assembles).

---

## 1. Who can make these

### Tier A — Prototype / low volume (start here: 5–50 units)

| Maker type | Why fit | Examples / search targets | Typical MOQ | Ballpark unit (rough) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Studio ceramic / stoneware artisans** (US Southeast, Scandinavia) | Your artist designs; short runs; Cherokee-country or Nordic makers | GA/NC/TN pottery co-ops; Finnish/Swedish ceramic studios; “custom ceramic bottle artisan” | 1–50 | $80–$250+/unit sample; $40–$120 at 50 |
| **Hospitality craft OEM** (marble / stone / ceramic drinkware) | Hotels already buy stone cups/jugs | BAFA Pottery (Jaipur) — ceramic, terracotta, marble drinkware for luxury hotels ([byartistforartist.com](https://www.byartistforartist.com/drinkware)); similar India/Turkey stone shops | Low–mid for trials | Quote-driven; marble pieces often $15–$60 wholesale at volume |
| **Italian bespoke ceramics** | High-end handmade custom | Ceramiche Delweiss (Italy custom) | Project-based | Premium |
| **US glass studios** (purple glass inserts) | Fused amethyst panels / rings | Local glassblowers; studio glass co-ops; “fused glass OEM” | 10–100 inserts | $8–$40 per insert |

**Best path for you as designer:**  
1) 3D model / render →  
2) One Georgia/SE studio for **stoneware body** approximating earthen stone →  
3) Separate **glass studio** for purple ring/blade →  
4) Food-safe inner glaze or quartz-look liner (true milky quartz lining may be inlay or kiln glass, not carved crystal — confirm with maker).

### Tier B — Small brand scale (200–5,000 units)

| Maker type | Notes | Public anchors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Custom ceramic bottle OEM** | Fully vitrified food-grade bottles/decanters for spirits | **Anfora** (Mexico) — custom ceramic bottles/decanters, MOQ ~**5,000**, mold development from ~**$2,700+** ([botellasceramica.com](https://botellasceramica.com/en/)) |
| **China / Chaozhou stoneware OEM** | Custom shape MOQ often **1,000–3,000**; logo/finish 500–1000 | Hosen, Brett, Storiware-class factories — flexible MOQ claims 300–3000 depending on complexity |
| **European glass houses** | Decanter/carafe customization | VDGLASS (Italy), EukaGlass, etc. — better for all-glass SKUs than stone body |

### Tier C — Real stone (heavier, pricier)

| Path | Reality check |
| --- | --- |
| Carved soapstone / marble / granite shops | Beautiful; heavy; chip risk; food-contact sealant required; per-unit often **$100–$400+** at low volume |
| Cast “stone composite” / geopolymer | Looks mineral; lighter; more manufacturable; still need food-safe liner |

**Recommendation:** Market as **earthen stone / mineral vessel**; engineer as **high-fire stoneware or stone composite + glass accent + food-safe liner** unless a Cherokee-country or Nordic carver can do true stone at acceptable weight/cost.

### Cherokee-country / Nordic partnership (non-factory)

| Partner | Role |
| --- | --- |

| Finnish/Swedish ceramicists | Nordic edition body |

## 3. Unit economics (tableside ritual)

**Assumptions (base case — validate with one restaurant):**

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Guest price (ritual for 2, keep 1 decanter) | **$280** |
| Restaurant keeps | 45% → **$126** |
| AquaQuarts net to brand (wholesale) | **$154** |
| COGS (mid) | **$110** |
| Gross profit before stewardship | **$44** |
| Stewardship / charity / source royalty | **20% of brand net** → $31 |
| **Contribution after give-back** | **~$13** |

**High case** (guest $400, COGS $90, restaurant 40%): brand net $240 → after 20% give-back and COGS, contribution ~$100+.  
**Low case** (guest $200, COGS $150, restaurant 50%): brand net $100 → loss after give-back unless COGS drops.

**Rule:** Do not promise “most to charity” until contribution after COGS is positive at pilot COGS.

### Refill economics (after vessel owned)

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Guest refill | $40 |
| Restaurant keep 50% | $20 |
| Brand | $20 |
| Water + booklet card COGS | ~$5 |
| Contribution | ~$15 (before small stewardship %) |

Refills are where margins improve — vessel sale is the customer-acquisition cost.

### Operating scenario — Year 1 (base)

| Metric | Base | Stretch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurants live | 2 | 5 |
| Rituals sold (decanter kits) | 80 | 250 |
| Guest ASP | $280 | $320 |
| Brand wholesale capture | ~55% of guest | ~55% |
| Brand revenue | ~$12,300 | ~$44,000 |
| COGS | ~$8,800 | ~$27,500 |
| Gross after COGS | ~$3,500 | ~$16,500 |
| Stewardship 20% of brand net | ~$2,500 | ~$8,800 |
| OpEx (insurance, travel, site, tiny ads) | $15,000 | $25,000 |
| **Approx. Year-1 result** | **Loss ~$14k** (investment year) | **Near breakeven / small loss** |

Year 1 is a **proof + design** year, not Maersk money. Profit shows when COGS &lt; $80 and volume &gt; ~400 kits/year or refill attach rate is high.

### Break-even sketch (steady state)

Assume contribution **$40** per kit after give-back (optimized COGS $80, guest $300, restaurant 45%).  
Fixed annual OpEx **$30,000** → break-even ≈ **750 kits/year** (~15 restaurants × 50 rituals) — or fewer if DTC heirloom sales at higher margin.

## 5. Sensitivity (what kills or saves the model)

| Lever | Impact |
| --- | --- |
| True carved stone at $200+ COGS | Breaks restaurant wholesale unless guest price &gt; $500 |
| Stoneware lookalike at $70 COGS | Model works |
| Restaurant wants 60%+ | Squeeze brand — negotiate kit as retail take-home sold through venue |
| Charity “most of money” before profit | Cash crisis — publish **% of profit or of brand net** after COGS |
| Import duties / breakage | Add 10–20% to landed cost |
| Local water editions | Cuts water freight; vessel still ships |

## 8. One line

**Makers exist in ceramic bottle OEM, hospitality stoneware/marble craft, and studio glass — almost nobody will pour milky quartz crystal liners at scale, so engineer the look. Financially, pilot year loses money by design; unit economics work if COGS lands near $70–$110 and guest ritual holds ~$250–$350 with a real give-back % after COGS.**
