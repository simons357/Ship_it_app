## 1. Does the quartz lining bring anything to the table?

### Honest answer

| What quartz does | What it does **not** do |
| --- | --- |
| **Brand** — name AquaQuartz means something visible | Change pH in a guest-meaningful way |
| **Beauty / art** — milky liner catches light; heirloom feel | “Infuse” healing frequencies |
| **Story fit** — Lithia water already formed through **blue quartz-bearing granite** (brand geology). The vessel **echoes** the rock; it doesn’t redo the aquifer | Turn high-TDS Lithia into Evian |
| **Food contact** — pure SiO₂ (quartz) is hard, inert, generally water-safe when clean | Dissolve enough silica overnight to reshape taste (room-temp dissolution is tiny) |
| **Hygiene note** — liner must be smooth, cleanable; rough crystal pores = biofilm risk | Replace a proper food-safe glaze if the surface is hard to sanitize |

**Bottom line:** Quartz lining earns its keep as **art, brand, and geology poetry**. It is **not** how you win “best water in the world” on chemistry. Do not sell “quartz-charged water.” Sell “water held in quartz.”

### How to build it (makers)

| Approach | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Quartz-look food-safe glaze / kiln liner** (recommended for scale) | Looks right, cleans, passes food contact; COGS workable |
| **B. True quartz inlay / collar / window** (not full carved liner) | Real stone moment without impossible BOM |
| **C. Full carved milky quartz liner** | Art-object rarity; heavy, expensive, almost never scalable |
| **D. Sealed quartz “gem chamber” (no water contact)** | Pretty; zero chemistry — fine if you never claim infusion |

**Pilot default:** A + purple glass accent. Call it **quartz-lined / quartz-look** honestly. Upgrade to B for hero SKUs.

## 2. Can we alter pH / minerals to make it one of the best in the world?

### Yes — but pick which game you’re playing

There are **two products**. Mixing them without labeling is how brands get sued or look fake.

| Edition | Job | Chemistry strategy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lithia Springs Edition** | Character, heritage, Georgia story, ritual | **Leave the spring alone.** Label as natural mineral spring water. High TDS is the point. |
| **“World sip” / House / some Place Editions** | Win blind preference when you want soft luxury | Source or **formulate** into the preference band below |

You cannot take Lithia (~2,300 mg/L TDS, high sodium), spike alkaline drops, and still honestly call it unchanged Lithia Spring Water.

## 3. Lithia Springs — what the spring already is

Public brand / wiki-class profile (confirm with current lab COA before labeling):

| Parameter | Approx. | Taste implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TDS** | ~2,200–2,300 mg/L | Strong mineral; often loses soft-water blinds |
| **pH** | ~7.3–8 (often cited ~7.4) | Already in a fine drinking band — **don’t chase 9.5** |
| **Sodium** | ~450 mg/L | Salty / heavy finish for many people |
| **Calcium** | ~130 mg/L | Mineral body |
| **Magnesium** | ~8 mg/L | Low vs “bright” alpine waters |
| **Lithium** | ~500 mcg/L | Story mineral — **no mood/medical claims** |
| **Geology** | Granite / quartz-bearing rock | Perfect match for Aqua**Quartz** name |

**Lithia recipe (locked):**

1. Contract source + story rights.  
2. Pathogen / contaminant testing; FDA bottled-water / state rules.  
3. Optional: UV or partner filtration **only if required** — no vortex theater.  
4. Serve **cold** (minerals taste softer cold).  
5. Pour into quartz vessel tableside or under hospitality SOP.  
6. Label: *Natural mineral spring water from Lithia Springs, Georgia* — minerals listed; **no** “restructured,” **no** lithium dosing.

## 4. The “world-class sip” target (industry preference band)

If the goal is water that often **wins** preference panels (NYC-tap / Evian neighborhood — not Gerolsteiner shock):

| Lever | Target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **TDS** | **150–400 mg/L** (sweet spot often ~200–350) | Preferred bottled/tap cluster; &gt;~800 often scores poorly |
| **pH** | **~7.0–8.0** (aim ~7.2–7.8) | Slightly alkaline-neutral drinks clean; extreme alkaline is marketing, not taste science |
| **Calcium** | ~40–100 mg/L | Round mouthfeel |
| **Magnesium** | ~10–30 mg/L | Soft brightness; too high → bitter |
| **Bicarbonate** | ~100–300 mg/L | Smoothness |
| **Sodium** | **as low as practical** (&lt;20–50 mg/L for “best sip”) | High Na = salty (Lithia’s challenge) |
| **Chloride / free chlorine** | Near zero odor | Chlorine kills luxury |
| **Temperature** | **Cold** (~4–10 °C) | Always |
| **Carbonation** | Optional light sparkle for dining | Different SKU — still vs sparkling |

Reference vibe: Evian-class (~345 mg/L TDS, pH ~7.2, low Na) — not “more minerals = better.”

## 5. Legal / honest ways to hit that sip recipe

### Path A — Choose a better-tasting **source** (cleanest story)
For Place Editions (Iceland, Alps-style license, Catskill story, Finnish spring): pick water **already** in the band. No chemistry homework.

### Path B — **Remineralized** purified water (hospitality “house AquaQuartz”)
Common in specialty coffee / tasting rooms:

1. Start with RO or distilled (flat blank).  
2. Add food-grade mineral salts to hit Ca / Mg / HCO₃ targets (commercial remineralization blends exist).  
3. Adjust to pH ~7.2–7.8 with bicarbonate system — **not** random “alkaline drops.”  
4. Label clearly: *purified water with added minerals for taste* (or equivalent honest wording).  
5. Blind-test vs Evian / local luxury still water before you brag.

**This is how you deliberately chase “one of the best sips.”** It is **not** Lithia.

### Path C — **Blend** (bridge SKU — careful)
Example concept: soft base water + a **small %** Lithia for place character without full salt load.  
Must label as a **blend**; get counsel; don’t imply 100% spring if it isn’t.  
Only if Lithia partner agrees in writing.

### Path D — What **not** to do
| Don’t | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Dump baking soda / alkaline drops into Lithia and call it Lithia | Misbranding; taste can go soapy |
| Claim quartz liner raises pH or structures water | Not true at guest scale |
| Sell lithium mood cures | Liability; not your lane |
| Ozone/UV theater as “energy activation” | Keep tech boring and safe |
| Claim a **UV / light burst** removes chlorine or sulfur taste | UV kills microbes; **carbon** removes disinfectant/sulfur odor |
| Hot-serve mineral water for preference wins | Cold wins |

### Taste cleanup (before the decanter)

| Want to remove | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Chlorine taste/smell | Activated carbon |
| Chloramine | **Catalytic** carbon |
| Mild hydrogen sulfide (“rotten egg”) | Catalytic carbon (worse cases: dedicated sulfur treatment) |
| Microbes on a private loop | UV OK as *extra* disinfection — **not** a taste filter |

Pour only **after** filtration. Vessel stays inert art.

## 7. Simple lab checklist (before you call anything “best”)

1. Full mineral panel + pH + TDS + microbiology on every source/batch.  
2. Triangle or preference test: your water vs (a) local tap (b) Evian or Acqua Panna-class still.  
3. Serve all samples same temperature.  
4. If House Sip loses to Evian, tweak minerals — don’t invent structure.  
5. If Lithia loses to NYC tap — **expected**. Keep selling Lithia as terroir.
