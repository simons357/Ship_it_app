# What makes water taste “best” — industry / sensory data

**For AquaQuarts:** Blind preference ≠ brand. Soft municipal water often wins sips; mineral springs win **place/character**. Use this to brief restaurants and source partners — not to invent structuring claims.

---

## 1. Big picture

Taste of clean water (no off-odors) is driven mainly by **dissolved minerals (TDS)** and **which ions** dominate — plus temperature, chlorine/chloramine, and organic taste-and-odor compounds.

Regulators set **aesthetic** guidance (e.g. USEPA secondary TDS often cited ~500 mg/L; WHO/EU allow higher). Preference research is often **stricter** than the legal ceiling.

---

## 2. Total dissolved solids (TDS)

| Finding | Source / note |
| --- | --- |
| Preferred bottled/tap samples often cluster around **moderate TDS ~200–400 mg/L** | Platikanov et al., *Water Research* (chemometric bottled/tap study) — preferred waters linked to moderate TDS + Ca, Mg, HCO₃, SO₄; **TDS &gt; ~800 mg/L** scored poorly for many panelists |
| Very low / demineralized water often tastes **flat, sweet, bitter, or “rough”** | WaterTOP / industry reviews; Dietrich & others |
| Liking can **fall as TDS rises** (~−0.23 liking units per +100 mg/L TDS in one utility-guidance study) | Dietrich et al., *Desalination* guidance on TDS & taste |
| Consumers need roughly **ΔTDS ≳ 150 mg/L** to reliably tell two waters apart (order-of-magnitude guidance; depends on baseline) | Same TDS optimization literature; older work sometimes cites larger Δ at high baselines |

**AquaQuarts implication:** Lithia-class mineral water (historically **high TDS**, e.g. brand literature ~2,300 mg/L) will often **lose** a NYC-tap-style blind test. Sell character + ritual, not “best sip.”

---

## 3. Which minerals help vs hurt

| Generally **positive / preferred** (moderate levels) | Generally **negative** when high |
| --- | --- |
| **Calcium (Ca²⁺)** | **Sodium (Na⁺)** — salty |
| **Magnesium (Mg²⁺)** | **Potassium (K⁺)** |
| **Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)** | **Chloride (Cl⁻)** — salty / harsh |
| **Sulfate (SO₄²⁻)** at low–medium (some studies) | Very high sulfate — salty / gypsum / laxative notes |
| Slightly higher **pH** (within drinking range) associated with better scores in some panels | Strong free **chlorine** odor (see below) |

Classic European salt-panel work (Zoeteman et al.): taste optima reported historically around **~270 mg/L CaSO₄** and **~90 mg/L MgSO₄** when added to distilled water (old but still cited); objectionable cation levels vary widely by person.

At high concentration, Ca/Mg salts can also read **bitter / astringent / metallic** — “more minerals” is not always better.

---

## 4. Chlorine, odor, and “tap vs bottled”

| Factor | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Free chlorine / chloramines** | Strong **odor** cue; many dislike it; panels can use it to tell tap from bottled even when overall flavor scores don’t collapse |
| **Geosmin / MIB / other organics** | Classic “earthy / musty” complaints — treatment/distribution issue, not mineral story |
| **Temperature** | Minerals taste **stronger at room temp** than ice-cold; cold water often preferred for drinking even if “flatter” |

---

## 5. Why NYC-type water often wins taste tests

Soft, low-to-moderate mineral mountain/surface water (Catskill/Delaware style): low off-odors, modest TDS, not salty — matches the **“invisible good water”** preference band. Expensive bottled water with odd mineral loads or marketing often ties or loses in blinds.

---

## 6. Practical checklist (source selection)

For a Place Edition you want to **win sips** (not only tell a story):

1. Lab panel or simple triangle test vs local tap.  
2. Target ballpark **TDS 150–400 mg/L** if chasing preference.  
3. Favor Ca / Mg / HCO₃ balance; watch Na / Cl.  
4. Serve cold for hospitality; disclose mineral character if TDS is high.  
5. Never claim structuring improves taste — **no evidence lane**.

For **Lithia Edition:** lean into mineral heritage and ritual; don’t promise blind-test victory.

---

## 7. Key references (starting points)

- Platikanov et al. — minerals & taste, chemometrics (*Water Research*)  
- Dietrich et al. — TDS discrimination & liking (*Desalination* / AWWA-adjacent consumer TDS work)  
- WaterTOP network review — taste & odor challenges  
- Zoeteman / WHO sulfate notes — historical salt taste thresholds  
- European mineral-water nutrient–taste regression studies (e.g. PMC7765973)

---

## One line

**Best-tasting water in industry data = clean, cold, low off-odor, moderate TDS with Ca/Mg/bicarbonate character — not maximal minerals and not “structured.” High-TDS lithia is a different product: character and story.**
