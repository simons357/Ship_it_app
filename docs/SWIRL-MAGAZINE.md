# Swirl, alignment, and what Navier–Stokes still owes

Jonathan Robert Simons
Prime Field Technologies
10 September 2026

*Magazine cut. About 800 words. Not a proof.
Full working paper: `docs/SWIRL-PAPER.md`.*

---

In three-dimensional incompressible flow, vortex
stretching is the term that can make vorticity grow.
Constantin and Fefferman showed in 1993 that if the
direction of vorticity does not turn too fast where
the vorticity is large — Lipschitz control of the
angle — then the solution stays smooth. Beirão da
Veiga and Berselli lowered that geometric cut to
Hölder exponent 1/2 in 2002. Those papers are
theorems as *ifs*. They do not estimate the pairs
they refused: intense vorticity whose direction is
misaligned.

That refused set is the swirl leftover. I name the
missing bound WRITE (6): stretching from those bad
pairs, on a parabolic cylinder, absorbed by a slice
of local enstrophy dissipation plus a lower-order
term. On those pairs the kernel is still
\(|x-y|^{-3}\). The usual integral inequality
recovers a cubic energy wall, which is too weak.
The estimate is stated. It is not proved. It is not
in the record under another name. Closest cousins
(Grujić 2009 and after) localize or weight the
*condition*. They do not bound the bad-pair
integral from energy alone.

A cylinder of possible singularity still needs more
than (6). Flux through the skin, at energy level, is
Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg as a *smallness* criterion,
not as an a priori from finite energy. Exterior
Biot–Savart is why localization papers exist; a
crude bound on that remainder is not absorbed as
the radius goes to zero. After a closed local
enstrophy budget, the right implication is Serrin’s
criterion on a smaller cylinder. That implication
is not a substitute for (6). If (6) were proved and
the skin flux were still open, the cylinder would
still be open. If the cylinder is already small in
the Caffarelli–Kohn–Nirenberg sense, (6) was never
needed *on that cylinder*.

On 8 September 2026 a construction of finite-time
breakdown with a *smooth external force* was
announced. Charles Fefferman’s official problem
statement has four alternatives. (A) and (B) ask
whether unforced smooth data stay smooth. (C) and
(D) allow a force and ask for breakdown. A forced
construction, if it holds, lives in (C) or (D). It
does not decide (A) or (B). It does not bound WRITE
(6). A picture of a vortex that spirals in is not
an estimate of misaligned pairs in the unforced
problem. I do not claim that construction. I did
not write it.

I have a separate note that Navier–Stokes with an
extra Ladyzhenskaya / \(p\)-Laplacian stress is
globally regular when the extra viscosity is
positive and the exponent is in a known range.
That is a different equation. Sending the extra
term to zero remains open. I do not claim ordinary
Navier–Stokes. Inverse-GCD matrix facts I have
posted elsewhere are not this leftover and are not
the Riemann hypothesis.

What I can put a name on this week is the map.
The Hölder \(1/2\) cut is Constantin–Fefferman and
Beirão da Veiga–Berselli. Bad pairs are what they
left. WRITE (6) is the right name for that remainder.
It is not proved. Axisymmetric flow with swirl,
keeping the \(1/r^4\) tube source, is the same
leftover as the field. A localized Hardy inequality
does not absorb all data. I dropped a change of
unknown that canceled the tube term; that cancel
was not the unforced path.

This is not a solution. It is the leftover, named,
dated, and not sold as a close.
