# Prime Field high-altitude hull — funding brief

**Entity:** Prime Field Technologies LLC · Savannah, GA  
**Date:** 12 October 2026  
**Status:** Planning brief for a first raise. Not a quote. Not a flight record. Not a contract.  
**Replaces, for this raise:** the volume-fleet story in [`BUSINESS-PLAN.md`](BUSINESS-PLAN.md). Those older figures stay in the pack as planning estimates. They are not the ask.

The hull needs a new name. The U.S. Army already uses TITAN for a ground station. No new name is chosen in this brief.

## How long it stays up

Public NASA super-pressure balloons have stayed up for 17, 39, and 46 days. The longest heavy scientific balloon flights on record are about 54 to 55 days. NASA has aimed at 100 days. That aim is not the routine result. Flights are ended on purpose, because of power, wind, or a safe place to land.

Helium leaks. Night and clouds cut solar power. Wind on the way up and the way down can end a flight. “Indefinite” is not a claim we will make.

| Stage | Time aloft | What success means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First flight | 1 to 3 days | Climb, hold, land where we planned |
| Pathfinder goal | 2 to 6 weeks | Same ship comes down in one piece and can fly again |
| Later, only if the envelope earns it | About a season, near 90 days | Still a planned landing, not an endless hover |

## What the money is for

One unmanned weather ship.

- Float between 65,000 and 70,000 ft, above airline cruise and above most storms.
- A small instrument gondola, loaded on the ground. No crew.
- Climb and descent arranged with air traffic control, because the ship is slow while it crosses the airline band.
- Prove three things: it gets there, it holds, it comes back.

The older picture was a swept hull about 480 ft by 200 ft, a 20,000 lb payload, a ceiling of 130,000 ft, and a 12-ship fleet. That picture is not this raise. At 130,000 ft the air is roughly twenty times thinner than at 65,000 ft, so the same load needs a much larger gas bag. We do not build that ship until a small one has flown and landed.

People board on the ground, and only after unmanned recovery is routine. A weather package does not need a person aboard.

## Who does the work

Prime Field leads the program, picks the module, and owns the customer. Prime Field does not build the envelope in-house for this raise.

| Part | Who |
| --- | --- |
| Envelope and launch | A firm that already flies stratospheric balloons. NASA’s scientific balloons are built by Aerostar and flown by the Columbia Scientific Balloon Facility team. That is the class of shop to hire. |
| Weather instruments | A group that already flies atmospheric sensors. A university group or a radiosonde maker. They supply the box. We supply the ride. |
| Airspace | An FAA path from the first design review, not after the bag is built. |
| Military | A customer for pictures, after a civil flight exists. Not the designer of the hull. |

## What it can be used for

Honest uses of this first ship:

- Weather, looking down from above the storms.
- Wildfire overwatch: cameras and heat, for the people already fighting the fire. Not a water or retardant drop from 70,000 ft.
- A science instrument a partner bolts on for one campaign.
- The same look-down pictures, if a service pays for a trial.

Later modules, only after the first ship lands on purpose: a radio relay if the power budget allows, then a second instrument package a customer orders. Crewed science is after that. A hospital gondola, a heavy payload, and a higher ceiling are separate decisions. They are not in this budget.

## Price

The old pack lists a volume unit at $13.0 million, a 12-unit fleet at $156 million, operating cost at $2.4 million a year, break-even at about $200,000 a month, and a 10-year fleet return of 17.8 to 1. Those numbers describe the big ship. They are planning figures, not bids, and they are not this raise. The pack already says not to present the volume price as the pathfinder price.

This raise is a planning band of **$2 million to $4 million**. It covers design, the airspace path, the balloon contract, the weather package, and one campaign whose success is a planned landing. It is not a vendor quote. A single scientific balloon launch has been described publicly as a low-cost flight compared with a rocket. Building our own reusable station-keeping hull is more than one launch, which is why the band sits above a bare flight.

Operating one small node after that, helium and insurance and a crew on the ground, is a separate yearly cost. Use **about $1 million a year** as the modest planning guess. The old $2.4 million figure stays with the big ship.

## Income, modest and labeled as guesses

No customer has ordered this. The figures below are a cautious year for one working node after the pathfinder has landed and flown again. They are not a forecast.

| Buyer | What they pay for | Modest year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civil: a fire agency, a utility, or an insurer | One region, one season of weather or fire pictures | $1.0–2.0 million |
| A lab or a science agency | One instrument campaign | $0.3–0.8 million |
| A military evaluation | The same pictures, as a paid trial. Not a weapon and not a fleet. | $0.2–0.75 million |
| **If all three happen** | | **about $1.5–3.5 million** |

There is no separate consumer product. Households do not buy the hull. The commercial money is the civil line: utilities, farms, and insurers. It is counted once.

A good year of about $2 million covers the $1 million operating guess and puts about $1 million toward the pathfinder. A thin year, with only one small contract, does not. Nothing here repays a 12-ship fleet or supports a 17.8-to-1 return.

## The sentence for a funder

We are raising $2 million to $4 million to fly one unmanned weather ship at 65,000 to 70,000 ft, hold it for days and then for weeks, and bring it down on purpose. Prime Field leads. A proven balloon maker builds and launches it. A weather group supplies the instruments. Income, if earned, is pictures and instrument time sold to civil users and, later, a military trial. The large hull, the crew, and the long list of modules wait until this ship has landed.

## What an investor is offered

The product is one unmanned ship that stays over one region and sells the view: weather, fire, or a science box. The return is fee income from that view. A good year in the guess above covers operations and starts to repay the pathfinder. A thin year does not. That is the whole offer.

Buyers already pay companies that fly this layer. Aerostar, World View, and Sceye have flights and contracts. NASA already flies science balloons. Our opening is a smaller node aimed at one region, at a cost per flying day we have not earned the right to quote yet. A model that pretends we are already cheaper than an airplane would be a false pitch.

A simulation we can stand behind, when we build it, uses published winds, a helium-loss assumption, solar power by day and night, and a planned landing. It prints days aloft and dollars per day. It does not show a fleet, a crew, or a buyer we do not have.

No famous acquisition is part of this case. A later sale happens only if the ship flies, lands, and has a paying user. Until then the company is a plan in a market that already has operators.
