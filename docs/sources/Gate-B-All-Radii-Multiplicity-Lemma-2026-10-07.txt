GATE B: ALL-RADII MULTIPLICITY LEMMA
7 October 2026

Purpose

This supplement supplies the analytic step used in Section 4 of
Gate-B-Source-Assembly-and-Concentration-Test-2026-10-07.txt.
It is a proof for every radius, not an extrapolation from finite checks.
It does not assert that the optimal Q exponent is 1/2.

Lemma

Let S_s={v in Z^3: |v|^2=s}. Suppose 0<a<b<c. Let f,g,h be
nonnegative sequences supported in S_a,S_b,S_c, respectively. Then

 sum_{p+q+z=0} f_p g_q h_z <= sqrt(3) ||f||_2 ||g||_2 ||h||_2.

Subsets of these shells are allowed, by extending sequences by zero.
In particular, if A_a,B_b,C_c are arbitrary subsets of the three shells,
and mult counts triples p in A_a, q in B_b, z in C_c with p+q+z=0,
then

 mult^2 <= 3 |A_a| |B_b| |C_c|.

Proof

Set F_k=sum_{p+q=k} f_p g_q for k in S_c. Expanding its squared norm
on S_c, split into diagonal p=p' and off-diagonal p!=p' terms:

 sum_{k in S_c} F_k^2 = D+O.

The diagonal satisfies
 D=sum_{k in S_c,p} f_p^2 g_{k-p}^2 <= ||f||_2^2 ||g||_2^2.

For the off-diagonal, let I consist of ordered (k,p,p') with
 |k|^2=c, |p|^2=|p'|^2=a,
 |k-p|^2=|k-p'|^2=b, p!=p'.
Write q=k-p and q'=k-p'. Cauchy-Schwarz on this SAME finite index set gives

 O=sum_I f_p f_p' g_q g_q'
   <= (sum_I f_p^2 f_p'^2)^(1/2)
      (sum_I g_q^2 g_q'^2)^(1/2).

Fix distinct p,p' in S_a. Every possible k satisfies
 |k|^2=c,
 k dot p = (c+a-b)/2,
 k dot p' = (c+a-b)/2.
If p'=-p, the two plane equations contradict c+a-b>0, so there are no
such outputs. Otherwise p,p' are linearly independent: equal nonzero
length and collinearity would force equality or antipodality. The two
independent planes intersect in a line; a line intersects a sphere in at
most two points. Thus each ordered pair (p,p') has at most two outputs k.
Consequently
 sum_I f_p^2 f_p'^2 <= 2 sum_{p!=p'} f_p^2 f_p'^2 <= 2 ||f||_2^4.

For the second factor, reindex I by (k,q,q'); this is a bijection because
p=k-q, p'=k-q'. Moreover q!=q'. Fixing q,q' in S_b gives the equations
 |k|^2=c,
 k dot q = (c+b-a)/2,
 k dot q' = (c+b-a)/2.
Antipodal q,q' are again impossible since c+b-a>0; otherwise the same
two-plane/sphere argument gives at most two outputs. Therefore
 sum_I g_q^2 g_q'^2 <= 2 ||g||_2^4.

Together these bounds give O<=2 ||f||_2^2 ||g||_2^2, and hence
 sum_{k in S_c} F_k^2 <= 3 ||f||_2^2 ||g||_2^2.
Finally Cauchy-Schwarz in
 sum_{p+q+z=0} f_p g_q h_z = sum_{k in S_c} F_k h_{-k}
proves the lemma. Taking indicator sequences proves the multiplicity
inequality. No large-radius assumption, lattice asymptotic, or numerical
enumeration occurs in the proof.

Application to the growing boxes

For each selected block a<b<c in the original note, take
A_a=A intersect S_a, B_b=B intersect S_b, C_c=C intersect S_c.
If n_s counts the occupied modes in all SIX boxes on shell s, then
|A_a|<=n_a, |B_b|<=n_b, |C_c|<=n_c. Thus the selected-pair count obeys

 mult(a,b,c)^2 <= 3 n_a n_b n_c

for EVERY positive integer box size r. With modal magnitude 1/sqrt(N0),
the shell norms are f_s=sqrt(n_s/N0). Dividing the last inequality by
N0^3 and taking square roots gives exactly the source scalar factor
used to dominate the positive selected modal sum by sqrt(3) f_a f_b f_c.

The original geometric coefficient K(a,b,c)>=5m^3, m=100r, now yields
 M_H >= 5m^3 (2r+1)^6/N0^(3/2),
 N0=4(2r+1)^3+2(4r+1)^3 <=358r^3.
At E=1, ||Q||_2>=M_H and Y<=256m^4. Using (2r+1)^6>=64r^6 gives

 ||Q||_2/Y >= sqrt(H)/(800*358^(3/2)), H=m=100r.

This is a uniform analytic lower bound on an infinite sequence of H.
It rules out every uniform exponent theta<1/2. It supplies neither an
upper bound at theta=1/2 nor an optimality claim for Q.

Normalization and numerical scope

Under integer dilation of a FIXED configuration, squared radii scale by
d^2 and the source coefficient C(a,b,c) scales by d^3. The shell l2 norm
of Q therefore scales by d^3, while Y scales by d^4; the quotient scales
by d^(-1). Energy normalization does not change that conclusion.

The obstruction above instead grows the number of occupied modes and
closed pairs as the scale grows. Its elementary lower bound holds for
all r. Finite-r ratios need not increase exactly as sqrt(H), and the
checker's three small sizes do not independently prove the asymptotic
exponent. Its role is to check the finite formulas and implementation.
