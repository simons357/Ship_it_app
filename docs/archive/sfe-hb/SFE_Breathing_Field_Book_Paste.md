## I. Introduction and Unification Crisis

For over a century, physics has been split between two brilliant but incompatible
formalisms. General relativity models gravity as curvature of spacetime, while quantum
mechanics treats reality as a probabilistic field of wavefunctions and uncertainties. Despite
experimental success, these frameworks break down in unification attempts—especially in
extreme gravitational and quantum domains like black holes or the Planck scale.

The Simons Field Equation (SFE) offers a new paradigm: one not based on geometry or
uncertainty, but on nested harmonic coherence. Rather than assuming mass curves

breathing coherence field:

\[\Phi(x, t) = \sum A \cdot \sin(2\pi f t / \varphi(x) + \delta )\]

Where:

- \(f\) are prime-indexed base frequencies
- \(A\) are amplitude scaling factors
- \(\varphi(x)\) is a spatial coherence attractor
- \(\delta\) is a phase offset operator

This field produces interference structures—coherence wells—where energy stabilizes,
identity forms, and force gradients emerge. Mass and time are modeled not as fundamental
entities but as effects of phase convergence within the breathing field. The coherence
entropy of this field governs its stability over time.

Unlike periodic harmonic structures based on integer multiples, prime numbers produce
