# It’s not static?

**Question:** after the Harmonic Blueprint / SFE “breathing universe”
picture was set down, Jonathan asked: *it’s not static?*

**Answer first.** The universe in ordinary cosmology is **not** a frozen
snapshot. Far-away galaxies get farther apart as time goes on.
Cosmologists write that stretch as a **scale factor** \(a(t)\) that
changes with time. That is expansion. It is **not** the SFE breathing
field — a sum of prime-indexed sines. We do **not** claim SFE-breathing
for the universe.

You asked a fair question. “Static” sounds like “nothing moves.” Nobody
here is saying that.

## Two different “changing” pictures

They share a feeling — *something changes with time* — and that is where
the mix-up starts. They are not the same picture.

**Ordinary cosmology (this is the textbook one).** Space on large scales
is not a still photograph. Distances between distant galaxies grow. The
usual name for that growth is the scale factor \(a(t)\): when \(a\) is
twice as large, those large-scale distances are twice as large. Hubble
expansion, and later the speeding-up of that expansion, live in this
picture. “Not static” here means **the scale of space changes**. That
claim does not need the Harmonic Blueprint.

**SFE breathing (this one is retired as load-bearing).** The public
Harmonic Blueprint field, archived as **SFE-PUB**, was written as a
breathing waveform:

\[\Phi(x,t)=\sum_p A_p\sin(2\pi f_p t/\varphi(x)+\delta_p)\]

a sum of prime-indexed sines. The book used that sum as a picture of a
universe that “breathes.” SFE-PUB is **retired as a physical law**. It
is a historical public formula, not how we claim the cosmos works. The
symbols \(A_p\), \(f_p\), \(\varphi(x)\), and \(\delta_p\) were not
given a closed, well-typed meaning in the fragments on file.

Same word *change*. Different object. Different job. Do not glue them.

The letter \(\Phi\) is used for other things in this lab too. Those are
other objects. This note does not mix them.

## What “retired as load-bearing” means

It does not mean “the universe froze.”

It means we do **not** rest a physics claim on that sine sum. We do not
say expansion *is* prime-sine breathing. We do not say the scale factor
\(a(t)\) *is* \(\Phi\). We do not promote the book opening to a
unification theorem.

If a real SFE-style oscillation were on the table, it would need:

- a **named observable** — what, exactly, would an instrument read?
- a **predicted number** — what value, at what time, with what units?

We do not have those. Leaving that gap visible is honest. Filling it
with “well, \(a(t)\) changes, so the field is breathing” would be a
fake close.
