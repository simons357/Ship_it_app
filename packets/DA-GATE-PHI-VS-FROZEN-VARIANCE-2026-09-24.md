## Exact ratio — EXACT

For \(m\neq\kappa\),

\[
\frac{\phi_\kappa(m)}{d_\kappa(m)}
=
\frac{m^2+2\kappa m+2\kappa^2}
{2m^2(m+\kappa)^2}.
\tag{A}
\]

Put \(x=m/\kappa\). Then

\[
\frac{\phi_\kappa(m)}{d_\kappa(m)}
=
\frac1{2\kappa^2}
\frac{x^2+2x+2}{x^2(x+1)^2}.
\tag{B}
\]

The cancelled formula is continuous at the shell: \(f(1)=5/4\), so

\[
\lim_{x\to 1}\frac{\phi_\kappa}{d_\kappa}
=
\frac{5}{8\kappa^2}.
\]

Write

\[
f(x)=\frac{x^2+2x+2}{x^2(x+1)^2}.
\]

On \((0,\infty)\), \(f\) is strictly decreasing. The derivative
numerator factor is

\[
n'd-nd'=2x(x+1)\bigl(-x^3-3x^2-5x-2\bigr)<0
\qquad(x>0),
\]

with \(n=x^2+2x+2\) and \(d=x^2(x+1)^2\). Unique corollary of (B):
on any comparable annulus \(a\kappa\le m\le b\kappa\) with \(a>0\),

\[
C(a,b)
=
\tfrac12 f(a)
=
\frac{a^2+2a+2}{2a^2(a+1)^2},
\]

independent of \(b\). The worst point is the inner edge. At the
shell, \(C(1,b)=5/8\). Therefore

\[
\Phi_e^{\mathrm{core}}
\le
\frac{C(a,b)}{\kappa_e^2}
D_{e}^{\mathrm{frozen,core}}.
\tag{C}
\]

This is stronger than merely saying both vanish quadratically.

\[

\[

## Energy does not pay the low tail — EXACT as a non-bound

For modes with \(m\le a\kappa\), \(a<1\),

\[
Y_{\mathrm{low}}=\sum_{\mathrm{low}}m^4\lvert a_m\rvert^2.
\]

On the torus, nonzero modes have \(m\ge 1\), so \(E_{\mathrm{low}}\le Y_{\mathrm{low}}\).
Multiplying by \(\kappa^4\) gives \(\kappa^4 E_{\mathrm{low}}\le\kappa^4 Y_{\mathrm{low}}\),
which is useless at high \(\kappa\).

Energy alone gives \(E_{\mathrm{low}}\le E_0\), so
\(\kappa^4 E_0/Y\) need not be small.

Ordinary energy control does not automatically pay the low tail.
This is a real residual, not a trivial estimate.

A schematic two-mode field (illustration only; not Taylor–Green)
with a frozen shell core plus a small \(m=1\) reservoir makes
\(\Phi_e\) low-tail dominated while \(\kappa^4 E_0/Y\) stays
order one. That is the geometry that threatens \(\Phi_e/Y\).
