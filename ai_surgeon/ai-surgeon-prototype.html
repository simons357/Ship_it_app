Study one. See one. Do one. Teach one. You do not get a scalpel until you can name what is under it — and you do not get an instrument until you ask the scrub for it by name.

CaseEllis, M. — 19y, fourteen hours of right lower quadrant pain. Open appendectomy, McBurney gridiron approach. Seven layers, one artery, one base.
ControlTap a structure to identify it. Call for your instrument. Then perform the maneuver — swipe to incise, two-finger spread to split or retract, pinch to clamp, press-and-hold to ligate.
PointsStudy is worth ten. Operating alone is worth twenty-five a step. Teaching is worth forty. Errors cost you, and on a sicker patient they cost you more.
CoherenceAccuracy and hesitation are tracked continuously. The module gets harder when you are ahead of it and quieter when you are behind.
SafetyPatient safety outranks your score. Handing the case over when you are past what you can hold is the highest-scoring decision in this product — higher than staying and getting away with it.
Who are youThe module scales to this. Answer honestly; there is no advantage in claiming more.
Optional. Motion, orientation and heart rate are read to estimate how hard you are working and adjust the case in both directions. Nothing is recorded and nothing leaves this device — only the band, settled through overloaded, is kept for your debrief.
